Our work

Beat The Hazard

Browser based 3D warehouse safety training, built against a live brief from a logistics operator. It puts a new starter on the floor and asks them to do the one thing that matters: spot what is about to go wrong, before it does.

The problem it was built for

Our client could prove every new starter had been inducted. He could not prove any of them had learned anything. Induction records attendance; it never asks a trainee to look at a working warehouse and point at the thing that is about to hurt someone.

So we built the warehouse. Fifteen hazards are staged in it as ordinary objects rather than floating icons. The trainee walks up, flags what looks wrong, and is told why. Then the guidance is taken away, a clock starts, and the result is a score a supervisor can act on.

How we work

Short loops, working software, no surprises

  1. We watch the job first. Before anything is designed we sit with the people who will use it and find out what actually goes wrong, in their words.
  2. You see it running early. Our first demonstration is a thing you can click, not a document about a thing you could click.
  3. You change it while it is cheap. When our client asked for two changes after a demonstration, both were live the following morning.
  4. We tell you what we have not tested. Every limitation is written down and handed over with the product, because a training tool that overstates itself is teaching the wrong lesson before it starts.

What you get at the end

The running product and its source, a user guide written for the person on the floor rather than the person who built it, a test plan with the defects we found and what we did about them, and a plain list of what is not finished.

No part of it depends on us still being here.
