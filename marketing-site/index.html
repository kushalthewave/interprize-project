The product

Safety induction that behaves like the job

Induction training fails in a predictable way: it teaches rules in a room, then expects recall on a floor full of noise, forklifts and time pressure. We moved the training to where the decision actually happens.

◎

Spot it under pressure

The trainee walks the aisles and flags what looks wrong. Reaction time is scored, because in a live yard hesitating is the failure mode — not ignorance.

◈

Decoys, not a checklist

On the hardest setting twelve deliberately harmless look-alikes sit alongside the real hazards — a signed and coned spill, a correctly stacked bay. Flagging one costs accuracy. It teaches judgement instead of pattern-matching a list.

✎

A debrief that names things

Every round ends with each hazard listed by name and location — "Aisle C, north end" — including the ones they missed, with the keywords a safety officer would use.

⌾

Runs anywhere

A browser tab. It also ships as a single 716 kB HTML file that runs from a USB stick with no network at all — because warehouse Wi-Fi is not a dependency worth taking.

⚑

Built for a mixed workforce

Bilingual signage, adjustable look sensitivity, invert-Y, reduced motion and full touch controls. Nobody is excluded from an induction because of the input device.

⛨

Sign in the modern way

Passkeys, Google, Facebook and authenticator-app 2FA — implemented against the published standards and pinned to their official test vectors.

How it works

Learn it, then prove it

Training Mode

Guided, unhurried, impossible to fail.

  • No clock — the trainee sets the pace
  • Hints escalate the longer a hazard goes unfound
  • Every find explains why it is a hazard
  • Location shown so it can be found on the real floor

Test Mode

Timed, scored, ranked — the assessment.

  • A countdown on three difficulty settings
  • Speed is scored, not just whether you found it
  • Three correct calls in a row starts a combo
  • Ends with a rank and a full per-hazard breakdown

From the build

Three environments, all of it generated

Every surface below is procedural: the geometry, the textures and the sound are written in code and produced at load time. There is not a single downloaded model, texture or audio file in the product.

The company

Himal Interactive

We build training that people actually finish — and remember on the floor a week later.

Honest about limits

We document what we have tested and what we have not. A training product that overstates itself is teaching the wrong lesson before it starts.

Runs on what people have

No headsets, no installs, no procurement cycle. If it does not open on the laptop already in the supervisor's office, it will not get used.

Built, not licensed

Geometry, textures, audio, the QR encoder, the crypto — written from the specification. Nothing we ship depends on a vendor we cannot see inside.

Himal Interactive is a student software company formed for the CET257 Enterprise Project module at the University of Sunderland. Beat The Hazard is currently in development against a live client brief from a logistics operator. The demo linked from this page is the working product, not a mock-up.
